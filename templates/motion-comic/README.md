# Motion comic layout references

Open these references before planning panel masks for Motion Comic Static or Motion Graphic Animated. Choose a layout based on the framing the scene needs, then record the reference and reading order in the storyboard. Use the examples for composition, crop, gutter width and balloon placement. Use the project's own art and dialogue in the render.

| Reference | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| [Two diagonal panels](Screenshot%202026-11-09%20at%209.52.39%E2%80%AFPM-optimized.webp) | Pair a speaker with a reaction. A single white diagonal separates two full-height portraits. |
| [Three diagonal panels](Screenshot%202026-11-09%20at%209.53.06%E2%80%AFPM-optimized.webp) | Use three consecutive beats that each need a full-height portrait. Parallel white diagonals keep the panels distinct. |
| [Tall right panel with a left stack](Screenshot%202026-11-09%20at%209.53.22%E2%80%AFPM-optimized.webp) | Give the opening beat a full-height panel on the right, followed by two spacious close-ups stacked on the left. |
| [Right stack with a tall left panel](Screenshot%202026-11-09%20at%209.53.41%E2%80%AFPM-optimized.webp) | Put two opening beats in the right column, top to bottom, then give the concluding beat the full-height panel on the left. |

Keep gutters narrow and white or off-white as shown. Size the stacked panels to preserve faces, gestures and balloons. For Arabic, follow the storyboard's right-to-left reading order and read a stack from top to bottom.

Reserve the final slots and gutters from the first frame of each page. Leave slots blank until
their cues, then reveal the artwork inside each fixed mask. Move the artwork within the panel
and keep its edges in the same place until the page turns. A full-screen shot has its own page.
